# Design principles — Memory Place

## Who it is for

Older adults living with mild cognitive impairment or Alzheimer's, together with a caregiver. The person is not asked to learn a new interface, remember a password or complete a level. The place and the memory do the work.

## What it supports

- **Reminiscence:** open questions about familiar places and routines.
- **Conversation:** the caregiver can read the prompt aloud and use the follow-up.
- **Gentle stimulation:** one memory at a time, no score, no timer, no failure state.
- **Familiar technology:** a Lens on SPECS, used in the room, with hands free.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **One memory at a time.** No dashboard, no list of six cards at once.
2. **No scoring.** No correct/incorrect, no points, no streaks, no leaderboard.
3. **No timers.** No countdown, no session pressure, no auto-advance.
4. **No red error state.** A wrong pinch does nothing; it never says "wrong".
5. **Large text and high contrast.** Prompt 4.4 cm letter height at 1.5 m in Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed for low-vision readers; light text on a dark frosted card for an additive display.
6. **Big targets.** Repeat, Next, Home, Caregiver and End are at least 22 cm and spaced apart.
7. **Caregiver co-pilot.** The caregiver can reveal the follow-up and read it aloud; the person can still choose.
8. **Privacy.** No recording, no identification, no personal data, no camera.
9. **Consent and control.** The person can repeat, skip or end at any time.
10. **Not a medical device.** No diagnosis, treatment or clinical claim.

## Why SPECS

- The place is the cue: spatial memory is stronger than a list.
- Hands stay free for pointing, holding a cup or holding a hand.
- The caregiver and the person can look at the same card.
- The room is familiar; the technology sits inside the familiar place instead of replacing it.

## What to measure in the preview

- Can a first-time viewer read the prompt from 1.5–2.5 m?
- Can they find Repeat, Next, Home and End without instruction?
- Does the caregiver note appear only when requested?
- Does the session end without pressure?
- Does the experience still make sense with the sound off?
